# Diagnostics
Our diagnostic system consists of four primary systems. First, proximity-dependent ligation (PDL), detects the CspZ outer surface protein by binding aptamers and generating a DNA product. This product is then amplified by Recombinase Polymerase Amplification, a highly sensitive isothermal method that enables rapid amplification. The amplified DNA is detected by the CRISPR-Cas12a system through the collateral cleavage of a reporter mechanism, which then provides a user-friendly visual output on a lateral flow assay.
## PDL
LANCET’s diagnostic begins with PDL, which utilizes single stranded aptamers sequences specifically designed to detect the CspZ protein. As the aptamers come into contact with CspZ, they undergo a protein-DNA binding reaction and are brought close proximity to each other by a single stranded oligomer bridge sequence. Only then are they ligated, and can generate a double stranded DNA target construct product. This product serves as a template for downstream steps of the diagnostic.

## RPA
Recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) is an isothermal DNA amplification method which combines a recombinase enzyme with a strand-displacing polymerase to rapidly generate new DNA copies. LANCET’s PDL assay generates a limited number of ligated DNA products, requiring amplification for reliable diagnosis from a small blood sample. To ensure the output from PDL can be easily detected, we incorporated RPA, allowing us to produce a sufficient number of copies for successful detection. Like polymerase chain reaction (PCR), RPA utilizes specific primers to selectively amplify target sequences of interest. We designed LANCET-specific primers to amplify products from the PDL.

## Cas 12
The amplified DNA from RPA was subsequently converted to produce a quantifiable output using the CRISPR-Cas12a DETECTR system. Our DETECTR assay consists of three main components: the Cas12a enzyme, a CRISPR RNA (crRNA), and a single stranded DNA (ssDNA) reporter probe. We adapted gRNAs and target constructs to first validate the DETECTR system, and moved on to designing LANCET-specific gRNAs and constructs. LANCET-specific gRNAs were designed using Lambert iGEM 2025’s CASPER software and Benchling.

# Therapeutics
## CRISPRi
For the therapeutic aspect of LANCET, we utilized a CRISPR-interference (CRISPRi) system, consisting of a deactivated Cas9 (dCas9) protein and single-guide RNA (sgRNA), to downregulate critical genes in antimicrobial resistant bacteria. As a part of LANCET, our system was redesigned to incorporate multiplexing, targeting the critical genes *Bb0250* and *Bb0841* in the *Borrelia burgdorferi* bacteria. sgRNA constructs and target constructs were designed to target the critical genes *Bb0250* and *Bb0841* in *B. burgdorferi*. To further prove the efficacy of our system, we moved to testing in vivo, using *Escherichia coli* as a proof of concept. We transformed (strain) *E. coli* with our plasmid including the dCas9 protein and sgRNA targeting the *rpsL* gene.

## Cell-Free
Our cell-free inclusivity initiative focused on designing accessible cell-free systems for usage in education and frugal experimentation. We were inspired by the TUM-Straubing team’s 2024 Bluebear Bio Project, and received the tardigrade plasmid they used. We were able to test their tardigrade plasmid in a more resource-limited setting since we did not have access to ultracentrifuges.
