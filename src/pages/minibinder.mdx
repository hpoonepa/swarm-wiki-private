# Overview

Minibinders are small, computationally designed proteins engineered to bind specific targets ([Figueiredo, 2025](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/minibinders-tiny-proteins-with-huge-potential-in-bioengineering)). Lambert iGEM generated and screened candidates against a Deformed Wing Virus (DWV) protein through a sponsorship with Adaptyv. Our [modeling writeup](/lambert-ga/model-design/#structure) reports binding to the tested protein construct. Whether a candidate inhibits infection or improves bee health requires separate validation.

## Justification

Initially, Lambert iGEM considered nanobodies, antibody fragments derived from camelids. After meeting with [Aashish Manglik](https://profiles.ucsf.edu/aashish.manglik/) at the University of California, San Francisco, we shifted our focus to minibinders and their computational design possibilities ([Bouchiba et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-0716-1855-4_17)).

## Target Selection

Our study focuses on the DWV capsid protein VP3. The capsid is the protein shell that protects the viral genome and contains the structural proteins VP1, VP2, and VP3 ([Škubník et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1615695114)). The [minibinder design page](/lambert-ga/minibinder-design/) documents our project work; the [modeling section](/lambert-ga/model-design/) presents the computational analysis and binding measurements.
