# Overview

**Inclusivity:** the act of ensuring resources and opportunities are available to everyone regardless of nationality or social status. This is a value that Lambert iGEM has historically worked hard to uphold, and 2026 is no different. Through Flowers For the Future, children's book translations, the Neurodivergent Handbook, and Exceptional Science, we have taken special consideration to ensure that our initiatives have a widespread impact across differences in household income, primary language, or disability.

# Flowers For the Future

Sustainable Development Goal number five aims for equal access to resources and education, regardless of gender. We believe gender should bear no weight in whether an individual is given the opportunity to succeed. To achieve this goal, Lambert iGEM is partnered with Flowers For the Future (FFF). FFF is a non-profit program where Afghan girls can continue their education and obtain their General Education Diploma. For these students this program is especially important because under Afghanistan's oppressive regime, they are not allowed to receive education past sixth grade, and for many of them, this program builds a bridge to higher level education. Like many other high school students, the participants are offered an array of elective classes to choose from; one such class is our Intro to Synthetic Biology (see Fig. 1). In our class, we teach our students key concepts within biological science:

- The process of central dogma
- How to write a scientific research paper
- Experimental design
- Bioethics
- Polymerase chain reaction and gel electrophoresis

## Curriculum

- **Week 1: [Introduction to Synthetic Biology & Basic Lab Skills](https://www.canva.com/design/DAHCWr88fbI/S-9khKty9_84UYdSq52hvg/edit)**: In this lesson, students learn about our iGEM team and how we use synthetic biology. In addition, we teach them what synthetic biology is and how they should conduct themselves in a lab setting.
- **Week 2: [Understanding Units, Dimensional Analysis, and Lab Calculations](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGhoqA7vw0/uQ628NneXXZ7CUhGk3u2dw/edit)**: Week 2 is about understanding how to measure data. This week, we teach students what units are used for scientific research and how they apply in the context of experimentation.
- **Week 3: [DNA](https://www.canva.com/design/DAHEgVnnwZw/w5xfvXXiMZxXNI7z-75NYg/edit)**: We introduce students to both DNA and its uses. Students learn about how base pairs interact with each other; they also learn about mutations, and how they can affect research.
- **Week 4: [Gene Expression & Protein Production](https://canva.link/h669ygsrrlno56z)**: Week 4 was designed to further explain the process that DNA undergoes to make proteins. We cover transcription, translation, and how proteins affect gene expression in biological systems.
- **Week 5: [Introduction to Genetic Engineering Techniques](https://canva.link/zicg6p9asmure4b)**: In this lesson we teach about how scientists perform genetic engineering. We first introduce them to bacterial transformation, transfection, and utilizing viral vectors for DNA production. We then end the lesson by teaching them about CRISPR and how it can be used to alter or suspend transcription of genes.
- **Week 6: [PCR and Gel Electrophoresis](https://canva.link/dyc2k71n5a9tcnr)**: Polymerase Chain Reaction is a key step in the gene editing process: without it, we would not have the target DNA sequences that we edit with. We explain how PCR works and how scientists obtain results using gel electrophoresis.
- **Week 7: [Intro to Cloning and Synbio Tools](https://canva.link/3vu0bhzcve8y2jp)**: This week focuses on how cloning works and the different ways scientists purify their cloned sequences. We specify plasmids and explain plasmid design as a key step in this process.
- **Week 8: [Exploration of Bioethics](https://canva.link/zgcf61nhli6z88x)**: Ethics is a key concern in synthetic biology. This lesson asks students to truly think about whether or not certain experiments are okay to implement.
- **Week 9: [CRISPR and Genome Editing](https://canva.link/npw5d4b3crbb50p)**: This lesson centers around CRISPR. Students learn the importance of this gene editing tool, but also its limitations. We also use a case study to show how it is being used today.
- **Week 10: [Cell-Free Synthetic Biology](https://canva.link/7ncv6g8peh87i0u)**: This week is focused on both the design and uses of a cell-free system in synthetic biology. Using cell-free systems can substantially reduce the waiting time and also allow scientists to better control their experiments.
- **Week 11: [Experimental Design and Data](https://canva.link/p9j6obmycegesdx)**: Week 11 is geared towards teaching students how to design experiments, then compile their data. We highlight the importance of control groups as a baseline for experiment validity.
- **Week 12: [Your Future in Synthetic Biology](https://canva.link/m4w55bou8bk6fje)**: We finalize the FFF semester by introducing career options to students. We also include ways they can currently work towards being involved in synthetic biology.

## Impact

By week 12, students are able to take the skills that they have learned throughout the course and apply them towards complex scientific concepts. Throughout the course, we challenge students with complex writing assignments that further expand their knowledge in synthetic biology and build critical thinking (see Quote 1). Lambert iGEM has taught 4 semesters of courses to over 110 students, and we have recently launched a BioBuilder with some of the girls from our class with the goal of enriching them in a scientific topic of their choice. At the end of the course, students are given the opportunity to express their thoughts on the class and give feedback on what we can improve. Many students told us that before taking this course, they had little understanding of synthetic biology and limited interest in science. However, by the end of the course, they discovered not only a genuine interest in science but also a desire to continue studying it in college and potentially pursue it as a career.

# Children's Book Translations

In an effort to expand the reach of *The Mite-ty Adventure of Bentley the Bee*, our children's book, we emphasized the importance of ensuring that the book is not only accessible to English-speaking individuals. To combat the language barrier, we translated the book into 12 different languages: Arabic, Brazilian Portuguese, Chinese, Dari, Farsi, French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, and Vietnamese, prioritizing areas that are highly affected by Deformed Wing Virus.

In highlighting these regions, we hope to significantly increase the effectiveness of our book's educational impact. By ensuring that our book is a resource that is available to children across the world, we increase awareness of Deformed Wing Virus and the importance of bees in the ecosystem. Sustainable Development Goal number 4 aims for quality education across borders: our team has made this dream a reality through inclusive storytelling.

# Exceptional Science

In today's status quo, the neurodivergent community is often excluded from opportunities to develop and demonstrate higher level thinking. Lambert iGEM is committed to delivering a quality education to our neurodivergent community at Lambert. To complete this goal, we have a program we call Exceptional Science! We regularly meet with the neurodivergent class at our school and teach them important science concepts through interactive experiments (see Fig. 3). We cover topics such as acid-base reactions, genetics, physical reactions, and differences in density (see Fig. 4). Kinesthetic learning (learning by using your hands) is our primary way of teaching the students because many neurodivergent people learn better when the body is involved. Movement is known to help organize attention, support memory, and ease cognitive overload (Snow, 2026).

We teach many scientific topics, however, some topics show up at a higher frequency than others. This is because we have identified certain concepts as being key for understanding complex science: genetics, chemical reactions, and physical reactions. Most of our genetics lessons focus on visualizing the structure of DNA or the composition of cells, including how organelles interact with each other to complete processes. We view reactions as the cornerstone of science as a whole; therefore, we include many examples of reactions and explain the specific processes happening. One reaction type we repeatedly circled back to was acid-base reactions; this is because cell operations are guided by acid-base reactions, and by understanding how they work, students gain a better understanding of the drive behind bodily functions. One goal we have for students in this program is for them to understand the science behind things they see every day. For this reason, we focus on physical reactions.

This year, we also included a game about honey bees and how Deformed Wing Virus (DWV) affects them. In this game, students act as honey bees and they have to collect nectar hidden around the area. In the next round, they have DWV. For this round they are not allowed to walk, and instead have to hop around to collect the nectar, simulating the difficulties that honey bees with DWV face while collecting nectar. Through this game we teach the students the importance of honey bees in our world and how DWV destroys colonies. In Exceptional Science! we aim to link positive memories to scientific topics, empowering students to continue to experiment and learn about the world around them.

# Neurodivergent Handbook

As an extension of this program, we further developed our "Neurodivergent Handbook" and restructured it into chapters with units that are more comprehensive for teachers and students alike. This handbook acts as a guide for educators and students looking for simple yet educational science experiments. We decided to add a description of each experiment along with the scientific topics that it encompasses, and we made a description guiding educators on what subjects to emphasize during their experiments. Our vision is for this handbook to increase the quality of scientific education for neurodivergent communities across Georgia, and make the process as seamless as possible for teachers.
