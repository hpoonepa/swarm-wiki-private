# RNA-interference

## RNAi Overview

Ribonucleic acid interference (RNAi) silences specific genes by degrading specific messenger RNA (mRNA). Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) is cleaved by the enzymes Drosha and DICER into small interfering RNA (siRNA), which is about 19-23 nucleotides long ([Dana et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28824341/)). The siRNA is incorporated into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), where Argonaute removes the passenger strand and retains the guide strand. The guide strand directs RISC to the complementary mRNA, which Argonaute cleaves and degrades ([Neumeier et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2020.526455)). As a result, the mRNA cannot be translated into protein (see Fig. 1).

## Justification

Lambert iGEM selected RNAi over other methods of gene silencing technologies due to its non-permanent gene silencing, noninvasive delivery, and scalability (see Table 1).

| RNAi (RNA interference)<br />([Boettcher & McManus, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2015.04.028)) | CRISPR<br />(Yang et al., 2020) | TALE<br />([Gaj et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tibtech.2013.04.004)) |
|---|---|---|

Following the selection of RNAi, Lambert iGEM compared double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) and short-hairpin RNA (shRNA) to determine the most suitable approach for our delivery system. We selected dsRNA based on its advantages for our proposed application (see Table 2).

## Target Selection

Varroa destructor relies on salivary enzymes to feed on honeybees, digesting host tissue externally before ingesting it ([Ramsey et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1818371116)). As a result, the mite's saliva carries Deformed Wing Virus (DWV): 9 DWV proteins have been identified in the mite's salivary secretome ([Zhang & Han, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30219905/)). Therefore, RNAi knockdown of a salivary enzyme gene can disrupt feeding and viral transmission while reducing mite survival by 40–50% ([Becchimanzi et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/imb.12945)).

To select our target genes, we developed a set of criteria that ensure our RNAi system is effective and safe:

- Targets encode for genes that are critical to the feeding function and survival of V. destructor
- Sequence is consistent across mite species with low mutation rate
- Minimal side effects to the health of the bee colony when our targets are silenced
- Sequence is unique to V. destructor

We accordingly applied these criteria to the genome of V. destructor, and considered several targets, eventually finalizing on the genes Vd-CHIsal, Peritrophin-A, and Vdav422 (see Table 3).

| Gene of Interest | Function | Effect of Repression |
|---|---|---|
| Vd-CHIsal<br />([Becchimanzi et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1009075)) | A glycoside hydrolase in the mite's saliva that breaks down chitin in the bee's exoskeleton (see Fig. 2). | Impairs chitin degradation, reducing the mite's ability to penetrate the bee's exoskeleton and feed. |
| Peritrophin-A<br />([Becchimanzi et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/imb.12945)) | Anchors proteins that don't normally bind chitin to chitin-containing structures, with minimal damage (see Fig. 2). | Disrupts chitin-associated structures, impairing mite digestion and nutrient absorption. |
| Vdav422<br />([Kim et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42102981/)) | Keeps feeding wounds open, suppresses the host immune response, and helps the mite ingest nutrients (see Fig. 2). | Disrupts feeding-site maintenance and immune suppression, reducing nutrient uptake and mite survival. |
