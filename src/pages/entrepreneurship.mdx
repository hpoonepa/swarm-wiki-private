# Entrepreneurship
 
SWARM aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 8, which promotes sustained, inclusive, and sustainable economic growth. Honey bee colony losses threaten the livelihoods of commercial beekeepers and the agricultural industries dependent on pollination services. By developing an innovative approach to managing Varroa mites and Deformed Wing Virus, SWARM aims to improve colony productivity and strengthen the economic stability of beekeeping operations. Through biotechnology innovation and strategic industry partnerships, SWARM also seeks to support sustainable growth within the agricultural sector.
 
## 1. Executive Summary
 
Honey bee populations face persistent threats from *Varroa destructor* mites and Deformed Wing Virus (DWV), which contribute to colony losses and substantial economic challenges for commercial beekeepers. Existing treatments primarily target Varroa infestations, leaving limited options for directly addressing DWV infection. Lambert iGEM developed SWARM, a dual-action biotechnology platform combining engineered minibinders designed to inhibit DWV with multiplexed RNA interference (RNAi) targeting essential functions in Varroa mites.
 
SWARM targets commercial beekeepers experiencing significant DWV-related challenges, particularly those supplying pollination services to high-value agricultural operations. Our business-to-business licensing model would allow established agricultural biotechnology manufacturers to produce and distribute SWARM through existing commercial networks. Separately, Vapor-X, our low-cost vacuum-assisted drying system, offers an additional licensing opportunity for educational and research laboratories.
 
At a proposed retail price of **\$39.99 per hive treatment** and an estimated mature manufacturing cost of **\$25.00**, SWARM is positioned as a specialized, premium bee-health product. Our commercialization model projects **300,000 annual treatments by 2034**, generating approximately **\$12 million in retail sales** and **\$525,000 in licensing royalties** under an illustrative 5% royalty agreement. Our long-term goal is one million annual treatments. Through experimental validation, regulatory development, and manufacturing partnerships, SWARM aims to establish a scalable biotechnology platform supporting colony health and agricultural productivity.
 
## 2. Problem and Solution
 
### 2.1 Issue

Varroa mites (see Figure 1), parasites that spread Deformed Wing Virus (DWV), are one of the greatest threats to honey bees. DWV weakens the colonies that beekeepers and farmers rely on for income and crop pollination, and it is spread by Varroa mites that feed on developing and adult honey bees. The virus can impair wing development, shorten bee lifespans, and weaken colonies. Together, Varroa and DWV can contribute to severe colony decline and even collapse (see Figure 2).

This critical issue is the focus of SWARM because beekeepers must manage Varroa throughout the year rather than through a single treatment. Mite populations can recover after treatment, and untreated populations can grow quickly within a colony. Beekeepers therefore monitor mite levels and apply treatments at different points in the season to keep populations below damaging levels.
 
### 2.2 Burden of Varroa Mites
 
Varroa mites and Deformed Wing Virus present a persistent challenge to commercial beekeeping because infestation can reduce colony productivity, increase management expenses, and threaten pollination operations. According to the Apiary Inspectors of America, U.S. beekeepers lost an estimated 55.1% of managed honey bee colonies between April 2023 and April 2024 [1], illustrating the scale of the broader colony-health problem. These losses arise from multiple interacting factors, including parasites, pathogens, nutrition, and environmental stressors.
 
Varroa mites further intensify colony-health challenges by transmitting DWV and weakening infected honey bees. Although existing miticides can reduce mite populations, resistance and persistent viral infections remain important management concerns. SWARM addresses these interconnected threats through a combined approach targeting mite feeding functions and DWV infection, with the aim of improving colony health and reducing the economic burden associated with colony losses.
 
### 2.3 Solution
 
Varroa mites are one of the leading causes of colony loss, creating recurring costs for beekeepers, and current solutions show resistance and inconsistent effectiveness. To address this gap, we created SWARM, a dual-sided platform combining a minibinder therapeutic targeting Deformed Wing Virus with RNAi designed to disrupt critical functions in Varroa salivary glands. By targeting both the mite itself and one of the major viruses it transmits, SWARM approaches the problem from two directions, aiming to reduce Varroa-related damage and improve long-term colony health. Since Varroa control requires seasonal treatment, the approach supports sustained demand and long-term profitability.
 
## 3. Market Analysis
 
### 3.1 Market Size
 
The global apiculture market was valued at approximately \$12.41 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach \$21.10 billion by 2034, growing at a compound annual growth rate of about 6.13% [2]. Asia-Pacific currently represents the largest regional market, accounting for roughly 36.12% of global revenue [2]. However, SWARM's potential market extends beyond beekeeping products because commercial honey bees are essential infrastructure for agriculture. In the United States alone, growers spent more than \$400 million on commercial pollination services in 2024, while honey bee pollination contributes more than \$18 billion in additional crop revenue annually [3]. This creates a large downstream agricultural market in which maintaining healthier colonies can directly affect crop production, pollination availability, and beekeeper profitability.
 
SWARM's primary business customers are agricultural biotechnology manufacturers, animal-health companies, and established bee-treatment producers seeking advanced biological treatment technologies. Our licensing model would allow these companies to manufacture and commercialize SWARM through existing distribution networks. Its initial end users would be commercial beekeepers managing significant DWV-related challenges, particularly pollination service providers whose colony health directly affects agricultural income.
 
### 3.2 TAM-SAM-SOM
 
#### Total Addressable Market (TAM)
 
The USDA reported approximately 2.81 million managed honey bee colonies in operations with five or more colonies as of January 2026 [4]. If every colony purchased one annual SWARM treatment, the theoretical U.S. market would represent approximately \$112.37 million in annual retail sales. This establishes the broadest domestic market within our initial geographic scope, rather than assuming that every colony requires treatment.
 
#### Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
 
SWARM's serviceable market consists of commercial colonies that could be reached through licensed manufacturing and distribution partners, particularly operations experiencing significant DWV-related challenges. For preliminary financial modeling, we assume distribution access to 60% of the national colony base, equivalent to approximately 1.69 million colonies and \$67.42 million in potential annual retail sales. This is a distribution-coverage assumption, not a measured estimate of DWV prevalence or confirmed treatment demand. Further beekeeper research and field data would be necessary to establish the actual clinically relevant market.
 
#### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
 
SWARM's projected obtainable market focuses on commercial beekeepers managing significant DWV-related challenges, particularly those serving high-value agricultural pollination operations. To account for the adoption barriers associated with introducing a premium biotechnology treatment, we developed multiple commercialization scenarios. Our base-case projection estimates 150,000 annual treatments by 2034, equivalent to approximately \$6 million in retail sales and 8.9% of our modeled serviceable market. Our more ambitious target of 300,000 annual treatments would generate approximately \$12 million in retail sales, representing 17.8% market penetration. Reaching this higher target would require demonstrated treatment effectiveness, regulatory approval, established manufacturing partnerships, and sustained beekeeper adoption. These scenarios will be refined through customer research and pilot sales data.

### 3.3 Market Competition
 
Existing bee-health treatments primarily manage *Varroa destructor* through chemical or biological methods. Established products such as Apivar and Formic Pro reduce mite infestations, while Norroa introduces an RNAi-based approach to mite control [5]. However, these treatments do not directly inhibit Deformed Wing Virus (DWV) infection, leaving a significant gap in colony protection. SWARM addresses this limitation through a dual-action formulation combining RNAi targeting essential Varroa functions with engineered minibinders designed to inhibit DWV infection. Its integrated approach distinguishes it from conventional miticides by targeting both the parasite and the virus it transmits. As the EPA emphasizes the importance of rotating Varroa treatments to manage resistance [5], SWARM could offer an additional biological treatment option while introducing direct antiviral capabilities currently absent from established commercial products.
 
Norroa, an established RNAi-based competitor, is currently listed by Mann Lake at \$22 per treatment or approximately \$19 when purchased in a six-treatment package. Each treatment covers one brood box, meaning larger hive configurations may require multiple treatments. At \$39.99 per hive treatment, SWARM occupies a premium market position while introducing an additional DWV-targeting minibinder component. This distinction supports its positioning as a specialized treatment for beekeepers facing significant DWV-related challenges, rather than a conventional mite-control product.

## 5. Financial Analysis
 
### 5.1 Pricing and Production Costs
 
SWARM's proposed retail price is \$39.99 per hive treatment, based on its specialized biological formulation and associated manufacturing expenses. Our estimated production cost is \$25.00 per treatment, consisting of \$23.00 in materials and \$2.00 in additional manufacturing expenses at mature production capacity (see Figure 8). These costs reflect the development of two active biological components, with engineered minibinders accounting for the largest share of production expenses.
 
Its premium price targets beekeepers managing significant DWV-related challenges rather than routine treatment of every colony.

Production costs are expected to decrease as manufacturing efficiency improves, declining from an estimated \$29.00 per treatment in 2030 to \$25.00 by 2034. These estimates are preliminary and will require confirmation through commercial manufacturing trials.
 
### 5.2 Beekeeper Return on Investment
 
For commercial beekeepers, SWARM's financial value extends beyond conventional mite control. Its engineered minibinders are designed to directly target DWV, providing a specialized capability absent from established commercial Varroa treatments.
 
To evaluate whether this additional protection could justify SWARM's premium price, we compared treatment expenses with the economic consequences of colony loss and reduced pollination income. Compared with a conventional treatment costing \$10 per hive [6], SWARM would require an additional investment of \$29,990. USDA data indicate an average nucleus colony purchase price of \$130 in 2025 and an average almond pollination fee of \$209 per colony in major almond-producing regions [7]. Based on replacement expenses alone, SWARM would need to prevent approximately 231 additional colony losses to recover its price premium. If both replacement costs and otherwise lost pollination revenue were avoidable, this threshold would decrease to approximately 89 colonies.
 
### 5.3 Revenue and Sales Forecast
 
SWARM will operate through a business-to-business licensing model, partnering with established agricultural biotechnology manufacturers responsible for production, marketing, and distribution. Although the retail price is \$39.99, we estimate manufacturers will receive approximately \$35.00 per treatment after commercial discounts. Under a proposed 5% royalty agreement, SWARM's technology rights holder would receive \$1.75 per treatment sold.
 
Our financial projections assume an initial commercial launch of 10,000 treatments in 2030, increasing to 300,000 annually by 2034. This gradual expansion allows manufacturers to establish demand before committing to larger production volumes. Our long-term objective is to reach one million annual treatments following broader market adoption.

SWARM's financial projections follow the expected development pattern of an early commercialization business, with initial operating losses followed by positive operating results as treatment sales increase. Our model projects that the technology rights holder would first achieve a positive annual operating result in 2031, while the manufacturing partner would first achieve positive annual operating results in 2032. Cumulative operating results would become positive in 2032 for the licensing entity and in 2033 for the manufacturing partner. ## 6. 4-Year Business Plan
 
SWARM's four-year commercialization roadmap (see Figure 12) outlines its transition from an experimental biotechnology platform to a market-ready bee-health treatment. Rather than establishing its own production infrastructure, Lambert iGEM plans to collaborate with established manufacturers that can bring the technology to commercial beekeepers. This approach allows our team to concentrate on advancing SWARM's biological capabilities while creating a practical route toward widespread agricultural adoption.
 
### 6.1 Year 1 (2027): Research and Development
 
Our initial focus will be validating the effectiveness of SWARM's engineered minibinders and RNAi system. Experimental studies will evaluate minibinder binding and antiviral activity, RNAi-mediated gene silencing, and the feasibility of delivering both components through a sucrose-based formulation. These results will inform candidate selection and establish the technical requirements for further development.
 
### 6.2 Year 2 (2028): Product Optimization and Safety
 
Following successful preliminary validation, we will optimize SWARM's formulation, storage stability, dosage, and manufacturing efficiency. Laboratory studies will examine potential effects on honey bees and relevant non-target organisms. We will also consult researchers, beekeepers, and regulatory specialists to refine the treatment's application requirements and identify the studies necessary for commercialization.
 
### 6.3 Year 3 (2029): Field Validation and Partnerships
 
Subject to successful laboratory results and appropriate regulatory authorization [8], SWARM will advance toward controlled apiary trials. These studies will evaluate Varroa reduction, DWV viral load, colony survival, and treatment performance under practical beekeeping conditions. Alongside experimental validation, we will pursue manufacturing partnerships, investigate intellectual property protection, and establish preliminary licensing terms.
 
### 6.4 Year 4 (2030): Commercialization Preparation
 
Our final development stage will prioritize securing a manufacturing and licensing agreement, completing required regulatory submissions, and preparing for commercial production. An initial sales target of 10,000 treatments would follow successful authorization and manufacturing validation. This stage would establish the foundation for expanding annual treatment sales toward 300,000 by 2034, with one million treatments remaining our long-term commercial objective.

## 8. Stakeholders and Team
 
### 8.1 Stakeholder Input
 
Throughout SWARM's development, Lambert iGEM consulted beekeepers, agricultural researchers, biotechnology specialists, and government representatives to evaluate the project's scientific feasibility and commercial potential. Our discussions with Peter Helfrich, President of the Metro Atlanta Beekeepers Association, and Olivia Menard, a UGA Master Beekeeper, highlighted limitations in existing Varroa treatments, including resistance, brood health concerns, and declining colony productivity. Their feedback reinforced the need for a treatment addressing both Varroa mites and Deformed Wing Virus (DWV), while prioritizing safety and practical application.
 
Our conversations also helped evaluate SWARM's commercial viability. John James, a hobbyist beekeeper, emphasized that an effective dual-action treatment would represent a worthwhile investment:
 
> "If there were an effective treatment that could eliminate both varroa and dwv like your project, I think that would be a good investment." — John James, hobbyist beekeeper
 
His feedback reinforced the importance of affordability and convenient administration, supporting our sucrose-based delivery strategy and pricing considerations.
 
Beyond beekeepers, we consulted researchers and industry specialists to refine SWARM's technology and commercialization approach. Dr. Jamie Ellis, Director of the University of Georgia Bee Program, supported our RNAi design:
 
> "Multiplexing is the better approach. The more targets you can attack at once, the better." — Dr. Jamie Ellis, University of Georgia Bee Program
 
His guidance reinforced our multi-target strategy and highlighted the regulatory testing required before commercialization. Discussions with a GreenLight Biosciences representative further informed our understanding of RNAi delivery, treatment resistance, and commercial development.

## 10. Vapor-X: Commercialization and Accessibility
 
### 10.1 Product Overview
 
Alongside SWARM's biological treatment platform, Lambert iGEM developed Vapor-X, a low-cost vacuum-assisted drying system intended to increase access to laboratory drying technologies. The device incorporates a Raspberry Pi Pico W microcontroller, vacuum pump, heating element, and environmental sensors to support controlled drying processes. With initial prototype component expenses below \$85, Vapor-X was developed as an affordable alternative for laboratories with limited equipment budgets.
 
Although Vapor-X was originally designed to support SWARM's research and formulation development, its applications extend beyond our biological project. Unlike the Varroa and DWV treatment, Vapor-X can be developed and distributed as an independent laboratory technology. We identified two potential pathways for expanding its accessibility: licensing the design to an established laboratory equipment manufacturer or publishing its construction procedures and technical documentation for future research teams.
 
### 10.2 Target Market
 
Vapor-X targets laboratory equipment manufacturers supplying educational and research institutions. Its primary end users include underfunded high school science programs, community colleges, smaller university laboratories, and independent research teams requiring affordable equipment for suitable vacuum-assisted drying applications.
 
Our initial commercialization strategy would focus on educational institutions with limited access to specialized laboratory equipment. Rather than competing directly with industrial freeze-dryers, Vapor-X is intended to provide a lower-cost option for smaller-scale drying applications where advanced freeze-drying capabilities are unnecessary.
 
### 10.3 Market Size: TAM, SAM, and SOM
 
According to the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), the United States had approximately 17,622 regular public high schools and 5,686 Title IV postsecondary institutions during the 2023–2024 academic year [9]. Together, these 23,308 institutions represent a theoretical educational market for accessible laboratory technologies.

Vapor-X's proposed advantage is the integration of vacuum-assisted drying controls into a compact, affordable system. Its intended applications differ from those of industrial drying equipment, and it is not a replacement for conventional freeze-drying.

### 10.8 Open-Source Contribution to Future Research Teams
 
As an alternative to commercial licensing, Lambert iGEM could publish Vapor-X as an open-source hardware contribution for future iGEM teams, educational laboratories, and independent researchers. This pathway prioritizes accessibility and scientific collaboration rather than direct financial returns.
 
Our proposed documentation would include a bill of materials, circuit diagrams, assembly instructions, microcontroller code, operating procedures, and relevant calibration and safety guidance. By making this information publicly accessible, future teams could reproduce the design, modify its functionality, and adapt it to their own research requirements.
 
This approach would be particularly valuable for high school iGEM teams and laboratories with limited funding. Instead of purchasing specialized equipment for every drying application, researchers could investigate a documented, lower-cost system built from commonly available electronic components. Any construction or use would still require suitable equipment, appropriate supervision, and verification of safe vacuum and heating conditions.
 
Open-source publication would also allow future teams to improve Vapor-X through modifications to its sensors, automation, and drying controls. Sharing these developments could extend the value of our hardware project beyond SWARM while encouraging practical engineering contributions within the synthetic biology community.
 
### 10.9 Future Development and Impact
 
Vapor-X presents an independent opportunity to expand the accessibility of laboratory technologies. Its commercialization pathway would prioritize manufacturing partnerships, product validation, and affordable distribution, while an open-source pathway would allow future research teams to build upon Lambert iGEM's hardware development.
 
Before selecting either approach, we plan to evaluate drying performance, reproducibility, equipment safety, and demand among educational institutions. Commercial licensing would require an agreement defining ownership and permitted use of the design. Alternatively, a documented open-source release would prioritize broad research access, although unrestricted publication could reduce opportunities for exclusive licensing.
 
By developing Vapor-X independently from SWARM's biological treatment, Lambert iGEM extends the impact of its project beyond agricultural biotechnology. Through either commercial manufacturing or open-source dissemination, Vapor-X aims to reduce barriers to laboratory equipment access and support continued innovation among educational and research teams.
 
## 11. Conclusion
 
SWARM introduces a specialized biotechnology approach to honey bee health by combining Varroa-targeting RNAi with engineered minibinders designed to inhibit Deformed Wing Virus. While conventional treatments primarily focus on mite management, SWARM's dual-action formulation addresses an additional gap in commercially available antiviral treatment options. Its proposed \$39.99 price reflects this advanced technology, with initial commercialization directed toward beekeepers facing significant DWV-related challenges.
 
Through a B2B licensing model, SWARM aims to establish partnerships with agricultural biotechnology manufacturers capable of supporting large-scale production and distribution. Our financial projections target 300,000 annual treatments by 2034, representing approximately \$12 million in retail sales and \$525,000 in licensing royalties under the modeled agreement. With a long-term objective of one million annual treatments, SWARM presents a potential opportunity to expand biological bee-health technologies while supporting commercial beekeeping, agricultural productivity, and sustainable economic growth.
