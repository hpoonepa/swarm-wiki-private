2. RNAi

Now, the mite that spreads it.

Our approach

Interrupt the feeding.

RNA interference (RNAi) reduces specific gene messages. We’re targeting three salivary genes in Varroa to interfere with feeding.

Our targets

Vd-CHIsal
An enzyme that may help keep feeding wounds open.
Vd-CHIBIN
A protein that may affect the bee’s wound response.
AV422
A saliva protein involved in mite feeding and reproduction.
Inside a mite cell

How RNAi works

An RNA guide helps the cell find and break down a matching message.

The process starts with double-stranded RNA that matches the target gene. Dicer, an enzyme in the cell, cuts it into shorter pieces.

These pieces are called siRNAs. Each one can provide a guide for finding the target message. We designed six candidates for our three mite genes.

Argonaute, shown in pink, joins other proteins to form RISC. Argonaute does the cutting once the right message is found.

RISC picks up an siRNA. Its sequence determines which messenger RNA the complex can recognize.

The two strands separate. One is released; the other stays with Argonaute as the guide.

The guide pairs with a matching sequence in the mite’s messenger RNA.

Argonaute cuts the message, and the cell breaks it down. With fewer copies left to read, the cell can make less of the protein.
